# Corpus Screening and Split

## Candidate retrieval

The initial Scopus search yielded 642 candidate records concerning waste-derived powders or ashes in cementitious materials.

## Title-and-abstract screening

Records were retained when they described an original experimental study involving concrete, compressive strength, and at least one target material family:

- waste glass powder;
- ceramic or brick powder;
- rice husk ash;
- sugarcane bagasse ash;
- palm oil fuel ash;
- wood or other plant-derived biomass ash.

## Full-text eligibility

An article was eligible when:

1. the target material partially replaced Portland cement or total binder;
2. an interpretable Portland-cement control was reported; and
3. the article contained sufficient mixture and strength information for record reconstruction.

Aggregate-only replacement studies, recycled-concrete powder or paste studies, and fully alkali-activated mixtures were excluded from the primary binder benchmark. Studies containing both binder and aggregate changes were retained when the binder intervention could be reconstructed; the aggregate change was recorded as a co-intervention.

## Balanced benchmark

The final corpus contains four articles per material family. One article from each family was assigned to development and three to blind testing, giving six development articles and 18 blind-test articles. The equal allocation targets cross-document and cross-material heterogeneity. It is a purposive engineering benchmark.

The complete article inventory is provided in `article_inventory.csv`. Publisher files are not included.
